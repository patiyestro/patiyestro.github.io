03 · Biography

About the Artist

IRADUKUNDA Patient, known professionally as Pati Yestro, is a Rwandan contemporary visual artist born on April 17, 1994, in the district of Rubavu, Western Rwanda.


His works are grounded in memories, resilience, identity, and history. He lost his parents at a young age and grew up with his sister, which shaped his understanding of female nature – care and pain in the same measure.


This, together with the experience of the Rwandan Genocide of 1994, influenced his art, in which he often puts women and children at the center of the narrative.


Pati found his talent for drawing at the age of nine and graduated from Ecole d’Arts de Nyundo in 2014. Since 2015, he has been exhibiting his works in local and international galleries.


He paints with oil and mixed mediums on canvas, creating surrealistic figurative worlds full of silence, memories, healing, and cultural codes.


His works contain flowers, clocks, spaces, and human bodies as drivers of reflection on a personal and collective unconscious, long-lasting pain, time, and resilience.

04 · Artist Statement

Artist Statement

My works are an exercise in memory, history, and human nature. I was born and raised in Rwanda where the Genocide against the Tutsi ethnic group happened in 1994. Painting allows me to make sense of what happened and its reverberations in the collective and individual consciousness.


I depict women and children in my paintings because I grew up with women, having lost my parents at a young age.


Women taught me their strength, their weaknesses, their resilience, and their capacity for suffering.


I paint topics of time, fragility, healing, memory, and space, using clocks, flowers, building structures, and human bodies as my vehicles. My works court the tension between universal and introspective, making the intimate history of the individual, their pain, and memories a collective search for meaning. Painting is my medium to confront, reconcile, remember, and heal.

“Change the world without talking, only using my hands.”

07 · Series

Current Series

Women Who Remain
Women Who Remain is a painting collection inspired by the strength, beauty, and resilience of women. Through expressive brush strokes, the series challenges societal expectations while celebrating feminine power and transformation.
What Survived Me
What Survived Me speaks about depression and struggle in a way that refuses the simple story of “I survived it.” Instead, it suggests that the experience was so heavy it consumed parts of you, and what exists now is what remained after that internal collapse.
